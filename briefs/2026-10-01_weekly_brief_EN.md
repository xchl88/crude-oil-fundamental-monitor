## Key fundamental developments

**1. Gulf supply shock (main driver).** The IEA estimates that more than 10 mb/d of Gulf output was still shut in during August. Global observed stocks fell 95 mb in August and 507 mb since February (about 2.8 mb/d). Hormuz transits recovered to 132 in the week of 21–27 Sep, against roughly 130 *per day* before the war. On 26 Sep, Washington rejected Iran's offer to reopen the strait within seven days. There is still no near-term timeline for physical normalisation.

**2. US balance: headline crude build, underlying tightness.**
- Commercial crude stocks rose 0.9 mb to 427.3 mb, 7.8 mb above the 5-year average for this week. Read alone, that is bearish.
- But the build came from refinery runs falling 0.55 mb/d (utilisation 92.5%, down 1.5 pp), not from weak demand.
- Products drew hard. Gasoline fell 1.7 mb and distillate fell 2.3 mb. Both are about 16 mb below their 5-year averages, and distillate is under the bottom of its 5-year range.
- Product supplied rose 0.45 mb/d.
- Net read: the crude build mostly reflects refinery timing. The US product balance is tight, and **WTI's discount reflects the US being relatively better supplied with crude, not a weak global market.**

**3. Brent–WTI widening.** US crude exports were 3.57 mb/d, below their 4-week average of 3.77 mb/d, even with an $11 futures arbitrage open. A wide spread alongside flat US exports suggests that barrels cannot move fast enough (freight, dock capacity). *This is a hypothesis; check freight rates next.* Over 2016–2024, Brent–WTI has been wider when Cushing is tight and exports are high. Cushing is currently 1.8 mb below its seasonal norm.

**4. OPEC+.** The September MOMR cut 2026 demand growth to 0.38 mb/d, from 0.60 mb/d. That is the fifth consecutive cut. 2027 growth was raised to 2.36 mb/d. The IEA, by contrast, expects 2026 demand to **fall** by 2.5 mb/d. A gap of roughly 2.9 mb/d between the two agencies shows how uncertain demand destruction is. In August, OPEC-12 output rose 0.35 mb/d (Iraq +0.66, Iran –0.40). A rollover of quotas is expected at the ~4 Oct meeting. Quotas matter less than the ability to export through Hormuz.

**5. China.** August crude imports were 8.93 mb/d: up 6% m/m but down 23% y/y. Refinery runs of 13.91 mb/d exceeded available crude by 0.64 mb/d, so China is **drawing on stocks** rather than buying seaborne crude. Product exports reached 0.98 mb/d and may top 1 mb/d in September. China is easing Asian product tightness while keeping its crude buying light.

## Trading implications

**Bullish**
- No timeline for reopening Hormuz, and ~2.8 mb/d of global stock draws.
- US distillate below its 5-year range, with gasoline and distillate both ~16 mb below average.
- Physical Brent premium to futures and >$20 Dec/Dec backwardation: the market is still paying up for prompt barrels.

**Bearish**
- Demand destruction: OPEC has made five consecutive downgrades and the IEA expects a 2.5 mb/d contraction.
- Chinese buyers are drawing on inventory rather than bidding for cargoes.
- US crude stocks are above their seasonal average; WTI M1–M2 narrowed $1.8 on the week.
- Any credible diplomatic progress would compress the backwardation quickly, given how far away from US fundamentals prompt prices are.

**Positioning read:** The risk is two-sided and driven by headlines. Structure (the time spreads, Brent–WTI) carries more information than flat price. Brent backwardation and a wide Brent–WTI spread are what tightness looks like *if the disruption persists*. Both would unwind first on de-escalation. Realised WTI volatility is 46% (20-day, annualised).
