# Crude Oil Weekly — Wednesday 7 October 2026

*EIA WPSR week ending 2 Oct (released 7 Oct) · NYMEX settlements 7 Oct · EIA Brent spot to 6 Oct*

**Bottom line:** Brent and WTI moved in opposite directions this week. Brent Dec rose $2.17 to $100.20 after China halted product exports and Iran kept Hormuz closed. WTI Nov fell $2.14 to $88.28 as Cushing built for a third straight week and the Nov–Dec spread narrowed to $0.73. The 3.2 mb crude draw beat expectations of a 1.7 mb build, but it is less bullish than it looks: imports rose almost as much as exports, and both benchmarks fell on release day. Products are still the tight part of the US barrel, but G7 and IEA releases aimed at diesel are capping cracks. The balance still favours Brent over WTI, yet the matched Dec spread of $12.65 is close to its widest since October 2024, so the risk of a reversal is growing.

## 3. Key fundamental developments

**US weekly data (week to 2 Oct): the headline draw overstates the tightening.**
- **Crude:** –3.2 mb to 424.1 mb, against an expected +1.7 mb. Stocks are still 2.7 mb (0.6%) above the 5-year average, and are essentially flat over 4 weeks (+0.07 mb).
- **Why the draw overstates it:** exports jumped 1.2 mb/d to 4.77 mb/d, but imports rose 1.1 mb/d to 6.84 mb/d, so net crude trade barely changed. Runs rose only 0.22 mb/d to 16.48 mb/d (92.7%), still below their 4-week average of 16.72 mb/d. The source's 'export-led' description is therefore only half the story. Weekly trade data are noisy.
- **Cushing:** +0.44 mb to 24.7 mb, +3.3 mb over three weeks, though still 1.5 mb (5.7%) below its seasonal norm.
- **Products:** distillate was flat (–0.04 mb) but stands 12.3% below its 5-year average. Gasoline rose 0.38 mb and is 6.4% below. Total commercial stocks fell 6.9 mb.
- **Demand:** weekly product supplied fell 1.10 mb/d. On a 4-week basis it is +0.7% y/y, with distillate –1.6% and jet +6.0%. Distillate tightness therefore reflects supply and exports, not strong US demand.
- **SPR:** –0.78 mb to 283.0 mb. A 40 mb exchange for Nov–Dec delivery is pending, with uncertain take-up (a June offer placed ~0.5 mb).

**Products and policy pull in opposite directions.**
- **China:** refiners suspended product exports beyond HK/Macau on 1 Oct. The source puts the loss at ~1 mb/d; estimates of the baseline differ by definition.
- **G7:** up to 100 mb of crude and diesel over four months, with diesel front-loaded. The US diesel export ban was put on hold.
- **IEA:** on 7 Oct it accelerated its stock release with diesel as the priority (single source, no volume given).

**Supply and geopolitics.**
- **Hormuz:** Iran says the strait stays closed until its conditions are met. OPEC+ kept November targets unchanged; quotas are not binding.
- **Middle East shut-ins:** the EIA STEO puts September shut-ins at 4.8 mb/d, down from 5.8 mb/d in August, and assumes 4.5 mb/d for 4Q.
- **STEO price path:** 4Q26 Brent raised $14 to $105, but its inputs were locked on 1 Oct, before the G7 release.
- **Physical vs paper:** dated Brent was $24.86 over Dec futures. This is a single EIA assessment, and part of the gap reflects the timing difference between a prompt cargo and a December contract. Even so, the physical prompt is far tighter than the futures strip.

**Brent–WTI: what is driving it.**
- Our 2010–2024 study links a wider spread to tight Cushing and high exports. This week Cushing is building, so the widening came mainly from the Brent side (the China product halt and Hormuz) plus WTI-specific weakness (Cushing, policy crude).
- On curve structure, the study's ~$0.03/bbl of backwardation per mb of Cushing deficit implies that a 1.5 mb shortfall explains little of the spread. The direction of travel (builds, then a flatter prompt) is consistent with the study, but this is an association, not a causal estimate.

## 4. Trading implications

**Bullish**
- Physical Brent stands at a large premium to futures, and Brent Dec–Jan remains at +$2.75.
- China's product-export halt and a closed Hormuz keep Asian and Gulf balances tight.
- US distillate is 12.3% below its 5-year average, and total commercial stocks fell 6.9 mb.
- The Gulf of Mexico storm threat could shut in offshore output (single source, details unclear).

**Bearish**
- Cushing has built three weeks running, and the WTI prompt spread is close to flat.
- Policy supply is large: G7 up to 100 mb, the IEA acceleration, and the 40 mb SPR exchange.
- Middle East shut-ins are easing. OPEC's 2026 demand growth is only 0.38 mb/d, and the IEA sees demand falling 2.5 mb/d.
- The market sold the bullish headline on release day: Brent Dec –$0.38 and WTI Nov –$1.16.

**Positioning view** (analytical, not a recommendation)
- **Brent over WTI (matched Dec):** fundamentals still support it, but it is stretched near the widest level since October 2024. Sustained US exports near the latest week's level, or any China or Hormuz easing, would compress it.
- **WTI prompt structure:** a further build would put Nov–Dec at risk of contango, especially with SPR exchange barrels due Nov–Dec.
- **Diesel cracks:** downgraded to neutral. Low stocks and China's halt are offset by diesel-targeted releases.
- **Flat price:** two-way and headline-driven. Volatility of 44.6% argues for small sizing.
