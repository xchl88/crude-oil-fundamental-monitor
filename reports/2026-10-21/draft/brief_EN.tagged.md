# Crude Oil Weekly — Wednesday 21 October 2026

*EIA WPSR week ending 16 Oct (released Wed 21 Oct) · NYMEX settlements 21 Oct · EIA Brent spot to 20 Oct · Price changes run Wednesday 14 Oct to Wednesday 21 Oct, same contract (Dec26 for both benchmarks; WTI Nov expired 20 Oct)*

**Bottom line:** US commercial crude stocks fell for a third straight week, by 3.1 mb{{F:crude_stocks_wchg}} against a 0.8 mb{{S:ev_20261021_13}} draw expected (Reuters poll). Supply did the work, not demand: output fell another 410 kb/d{{F:production_wchg}} to 13.27 mb/d{{F:production}}, imports slipped, runs were flat, and Cushing built for a 5th straight week{{F:cushing_stocks_streak}} to 27.6 mb{{F:cushing_stocks}}, 10.9%{{F:cushing_stocks_vs_5yr_pct}} above its 5-year average. Flat price followed Hormuz, not the US data. Brent Dec rose $2.76{{F:brent_m1_wchg}} to $108.82{{F:brent_m1}} and WTI Dec rose $3.25{{F:wti_m1_wchg}} to $97.15{{F:wti_m1}}; more than all of Brent's weekly gain came on the 16 Oct escalation, and mediation talk on 20 Oct trimmed it. Prompt spreads firmed on both benchmarks, and in WTI that runs against Cushing's level. Products went the other way: the ULSD crack fell $10.54{{F:ulsd_crack_wchg}} as distillate stocks built and Russian, Chinese and G7 diesel supply returns.

## 3. Key fundamental developments

**US weekly data (week to 16 Oct): headline tight, Cushing loose.**
- **Crude stocks:** –3.1 mb{{F:crude_stocks_wchg}} to 412.5 mb{{F:crude_stocks}}, 14.0 mb{{F:crude_stocks_vs_5yr}} (3.3%{{F:crude_stocks_vs_5yr_pct}}) below the 5-year average. That is 3 straight draws{{F:crude_stocks_streak}} and –14.8 mb{{F:crude_stocks_chg_3w}} over three weeks.
- **Why it drew:**
  - Production fell to 13.27 mb/d{{F:production}}, well below its 4-week average of 13.72 mb/d{{F:production_4wk}}. The source attributes the draw to lower output; continued Isaias after-effects (1.28 mb/d{{S:ev_20261008_5}} shut in at the peak) are the likely reason, though our sources do not confirm restart status.
  - Imports fell to 5.76 mb/d{{F:imports}}.
  - Runs barely moved: 16.65 mb/d{{F:refinery_runs}} (+34 kb/d{{F:refinery_runs_wchg}}), utilisation 93.4%{{F:utilisation}}. So this is not a demand-pull draw.
  - Exports fell 1.13 mb/d{{F:exports_wchg}} to 3.44 mb/d{{F:exports}}, against an October average of 4.26 mb/d{{F:exports_avg_2026_10}}. Lower exports keep barrels at home, so they **cushioned** the draw rather than causing it. Exports fell even with the Brent–WTI arb wide; our sources do not explain why (weather or port disruption is possible but unconfirmed), and weekly export data are volatile.
- **Cushing:** +1.0 mb{{F:cushing_stocks_wchg}} to 27.6 mb{{F:cushing_stocks}}, +3.8 mb{{F:cushing_stocks_chg_4w}} over four weeks and 2.7 mb{{F:cushing_stocks_vs_5yr}} above normal.
- **Products:** Gasoline –3.0 mb{{F:gasoline_stocks_wchg}}, 5.0%{{F:gasoline_stocks_vs_5yr_pct}} below average. Distillate +1.7 mb{{F:distillate_stocks_wchg}} to 104.3 mb{{F:distillate_stocks}}, still 9.3%{{F:distillate_stocks_vs_5yr_pct}} below average. Total commercial stocks fell 8.5 mb{{S:wpsr}}; the SPR slipped 0.3 mb{{F:spr_stocks_wchg}}.
- **Demand:** Four-week product supplied averaged 21.1 mb/d{{S:wpsr}}, up 3.2%{{S:wpsr}} y/y; four-week distillate demand was down 2.4%{{S:wpsr}} y/y. US demand is not the weak spot; diesel is.
- **Read against our study:** Cushing's level is the strongest inventory link to WTI M1–M2 (stock deviations explain 36%{{S:analysis}} of spread variation; about $0.029/bbl{{S:analysis}} per mb versus normal). A Dec–Jan backwardation of $1.69{{F:wti_m1m2}} with Cushing above normal sits outside that pattern. Our inference, not a tested result: national stocks below average, supply losses on the Gulf Coast and Brent's firm structure are holding WTI's prompt up. The study's finding that a wide Brent–WTI goes with tight Cushing and high exports also does not fit now; the spread is being set by the Brent leg.

**Geopolitics: Hormuz escalation, then mediation.**
- Iran attacked at least 9{{S:ev_20261016_10}} commercial ships after 11 Oct; Brent touched $110{{S:ev_20261016_10}} on 16 Oct, its first time since May. Kpler counts at least 18{{S:ev_20261020_12}} vessels attacked in two weeks.
- Kpler put Hormuz exports at 8.5 mb/d{{S:ev_20261014_8}} in the week to 12 Oct, nearly 40%{{S:ev_20261014_8}} below pre-war. That compares with IEA Gulf crude exports of 13.9 mb/d{{S:op_15}} in September; the two measures are not comparable, and the October reversal rests mainly on Kpler.
- Houthis claimed attacks on Aramco's Yanbu site and the Shaybah field (18 Oct). Saudi authorities did not confirm; treat as unverified, but it extends risk to the Red Sea outlet.
- On 20 Oct Iran's president met Pakistan's army chief on mediation; Brent Dec fell $0.76{{S:ev_20261020_12}} that day. The US no-attack pledge runs to the 3 Nov midterms.
- Prompt structure confirms physical tightness: Brent Dec–Jan widened to $3.75{{F:brent_m1m2}}. But the physical premium has narrowed sharply to $6.43{{F:brent_spot_premium}}, suggesting futures have caught up with physical rather than physical pulling further ahead.

**Products: diesel supply returning on three fronts.**
- The US suspension of sanctions lets Russian diesel cargoes return to usual buyers (20 Oct).
- The G7 release (up to 100 mb{{S:ev_20261002_1}} of crude and diesel) is front-loaded in diesel.
- Chinese exports resumed, though the October quota of ~3.7 Mt{{S:cn_8}} is below estimated September exports of ~4.36 Mt{{S:cn_8}}.
- The IEA attributes 25%{{S:op_14}} of its 2026 demand drop to diesel. Part of the crack fall is mechanical (the crude leg rose).

**Global balance (unchanged since last week):** IEA sees 2026 demand at –2.6 mb/d{{S:op_14}}, OPEC at +0.2 mb/d{{S:op_12}}. IEA on-land stocks fell 113 mb{{S:op_16}} in September, offset by oil on water. Brent now trades above the EIA STEO's 4Q26 forecast of $105{{S:op_7}}, which assumes Middle East shut-ins of 4.5 mb/d{{S:op_9}}.

## 4. Trading implications

**Bullish**
- Hormuz flows low and attacks rising; unverified risk to Yanbu.
- US crude 3.3%{{F:crude_stocks_vs_5yr_pct}} below its 5-year average after 3 draws{{F:crude_stocks_streak}}; Gulf of Mexico output still depressed.
- Brent Dec–Jan at $3.75{{F:brent_m1m2}} and rising; global on-land stocks very low.
- Distillate still 9.3%{{F:distillate_stocks_vs_5yr_pct}} below average.

**Bearish**
- Cushing 10.9%{{F:cushing_stocks_vs_5yr_pct}} above normal after 5 builds{{F:cushing_stocks_streak}}; low exports leave more crude at home.
- Gulf of Mexico restarts will eventually reverse the supply-driven draws.
- Mediation talk and the pre-midterm no-attack pledge cap near-term escalation risk.
- Diesel supply returning from Russia, China and G7 stocks; cracks falling fast.
- Demand estimates keep falling (IEA –2.6 mb/d{{S:op_14}}).

**Positioning view** (analytical, not a recommendation):
- **WTI structure:** Dec–Jan at $1.69{{F:wti_m1m2}} is back to its 30 Sep level of $1.69{{F:wti_m1m2_20260930}}. We have been wrong on it two weeks running and lower our conviction. The bearish case now needs both a production rebound and a 6th Cushing build; until then the spread is likely to track Brent's structure.
- **Brent–WTI ($11.67{{F:brent_wti_dec}}):** Two-way. Hormuz supports the Brent leg; depressed US output supports WTI. A recovery in exports from 3.44 mb/d{{F:exports}} would tighten the US side and argue for narrowing; restored Gulf of Mexico output would argue for widening.
- **Cracks:** Bearish momentum on diesel supply, but a 9.3%{{F:distillate_stocks_vs_5yr_pct}} stock deficit and seasonal demand limit how far it can run. Chinese volumes below quota would be the first sign of a floor.
- **Flat price:** Headline-driven, with vol at 38.2%{{F:wti_vol_20d}}. US data matter less than Hormuz and the mediation track; the risk window reopens after 3 Nov.
