# Crude Oil Weekly — Wednesday 2026-09-30

*As of: NYMEX settlements on 2026-09-30; EIA Weekly Petroleum Status Report for the week ending 2026-09-25; sources dated on or before 2026-09-30.*

**Bottom line: Comparing the same contracts week on week, Brent Dec26 was flat at $98.03 (−$0.09), while WTI Nov26 fell $1.74 to $90.42. That took the matched Dec26 Brent–WTI spread to $9.61, its widest since October 2024. The 0.9 mb US crude build comes from lower refinery runs, not from a glut: runs fell 554 kb/d, total commercial petroleum stocks fell 7.0 mb, and distillates sit 13.7% below the 5-year average. Prompt Brent is still acutely tight, with Nov26 expiring $5.50 over Dec26 and EIA's Brent spot assessment $11.37 above Nov futures. Even so, forward structure is softening in both benchmarks, most sharply in WTI as Cushing rebuilds, and US policy risk (a possible diesel export ban and a 40 mb SPR exchange) weighs on WTI more than on Brent.**

## 3. Key fundamental developments

- **The headline crude build is driven by refinery runs.** Commercial crude rose 0.9 mb to 427.3 mb, a second consecutive build (2 weeks; +3.9 mb over two weeks). Stocks stand 7.5 mb (1.8%) above the 5-year average. Trade flows pointed the other way: imports fell 179 kb/d to 5.7 mb/d and exports rose 289 kb/d to 3.57 mb/d, and both changes would normally draw stocks. The build therefore traces to runs, which fell to 16.26 mb/d at 92.5% utilisation (−1.5 pp), against a 4-week average of 95.3%. The EIA gives no cause. Autumn maintenance is the usual explanation, but none of our sources confirms it. The SPR fell 0.8 mb.
- **Cushing is rebuilding, and the WTI spread is following.** Cushing stocks rose 0.55 mb to 24.3 mb, up 2.8 mb over two weeks. They remain 1.4 mb (5.6%) below the 5-year average. Our 2010–2024 study links WTI M1–M2 mainly to stock *levels* versus normal: seasonal deviations explain 36% of spread variation, while weekly changes show R² 0.03. Below-normal Cushing therefore still fits backwardation, but the slide in Nov–Dec coinciding with the rebuild is consistent with that relationship. These are associations, not causal estimates. The study's sensitivity (about $0.029/bbl per mb below normal) explains only a small part of today's backwardation, which is mostly a global story.
- **Products are tight and the cracks show it.** Distillates fell 2.3 mb to 105.2 mb, 16.7 mb below the 5-year average. Gasoline fell 1.7 mb and sits 6.8% below normal. Total commercial petroleum stocks fell 7.0 mb. Demand looks firm:
  - Product supplied rose to 21.5 mb/d.
  - Four-week distillate demand is up 5.2% y/y and jet up 6.5%.
  - Lower runs cut distillate output to 5.0 mb/d.

  The ULSD crack gained $4.03 even though diesel-ban headlines are bearish for US diesel in principle. We cannot separate whether the market is discounting a ban or simply pricing scarce stocks.
- **US policy risk is concentrated on WTI.**
  - *Diesel export ban.* President Trump backed keeping diesel at home (22 Sep). Politico reported a 90-day export-ban plan, which the White House denied as a flat ban; Oct ULSD fell about 4% that day. On 28 Sep, Trump was reported to be considering a ban 'very seriously'. Reporting is fragmented and nothing has been decided. A ban would likely cut Gulf Coast runs, and with them US crude demand.
  - *SPR exchange.* The DOE is soliciting a 40 mb SPR exchange of sour crude from Bryan Mound and Big Hill for Nov–Dec delivery. Uptake is uncertain: only about 0.5 mb of the June offer was taken.
- **Geopolitics gave two-way risk.** Houthi strikes (intercepted) and reports of US–Iran talks coincided with Brent Nov falling 2.1% to $104.30 on 25 Sep. The US then rejected Iran's proposal to reopen Hormuz within 7 days, and Brent Nov rose about 3% in Asian trading on 28 Sep. Kpler puts Middle East exports at 12.8 mb/d in Sep, versus about 17 mb/d pre-war; this is a single source.
- **Global backdrop.**
  - The IEA reported global observed stocks fell 95 mb in August, with 507 mb drawn from Feb to Aug.
  - Demand forecasts diverge sharply: the IEA projects 2026 demand at −2.5 mb/d, while OPEC projects +0.38 mb/d.
  - China's August crude imports were 8.93 mb/d, against runs of 13.91 mb/d, implying a stock draw of 0.64 mb/d.
  - China's NBS manufacturing PMI was 50.1.

## 4. Trading implications

**Bullish**
- Prompt physical scarcity: the Nov–Dec expiry spread was $5.50 and the Brent spot assessment stood $11.37 over futures.
- The diplomatic path on Hormuz has been pushed back, and Middle East exports remain well below pre-war levels.
- US distillate stocks are 13.7% below normal while demand is firm, and the IEA shows large global draws.
- China's stock draws may eventually require restocking imports; this is speculative.

**Bearish**
- WTI curve structure is unwinding as Cushing rebuilds, and WTI Dec/Dec fell $3.05.
- A diesel export ban and the SPR exchange are both direct risks to US crude demand and Gulf Coast sour balances.
- Middle East flows are recovering, and Brent Dec/Dec eased $1.95.
- The IEA's demand contraction points to demand destruction.

**Positioning view (analytical, not a recommendation):** The market is pricing a two-tier picture. The physical prompt is extremely tight, while deferred spreads are deflating. WTI looks more exposed than Brent because three US-specific risks are stacking up: Cushing builds, the SPR exchange, and the diesel-ban threat. Our study associates a wide Brent–WTI spread with tight Cushing and high exports, but this widening instead came with Cushing building and exports below the April–May averages of 5.15–5.23 mb/d. That makes it look like WTI-specific weakness, which a policy reversal could unwind quickly. With 20-day volatility at 44.8%, headline risk on Hormuz and the diesel ban dominates single-week inventory signals.
