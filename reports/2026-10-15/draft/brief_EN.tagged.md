# Crude Oil Weekly — Thursday 15 October 2026

*EIA WPSR week ending 9 Oct (released Thu 15 Oct, delayed by Columbus Day) · NYMEX settlements 15 Oct · EIA Brent spot to 14 Oct · Price changes run Thursday 8 Oct to Thursday 15 Oct, same contract*

**Bottom line:** US crude stocks fell 8.6 mb{{F:crude_stocks_wchg}}, against a 0.3 mb{{S:ev_20261015_13}} draw expected (Reuters poll). Most of that is a Hurricane Isaias artefact: output and imports fell, demand did not rise, and Cushing built for 4 straight weeks{{F:cushing_stocks_streak}}. WTI Nov still rallied $6.48{{F:wti_m1_wchg}}, against $1.64{{F:brent_m1_wchg}} for Brent Dec. As a result, matched Dec Brent–WTI fell $4.11{{F:brent_wti_dec_wchg}} to $9.42{{F:brent_wti_dec}}, after peaking at $13.79{{F:brent_wti_dec_max}} on 12 Oct. Products moved the other way: the 3-2-1 crack fell $10.80{{F:crack_321_wchg}} as China resumed fuel exports and gasoline built. Brent's support now rests on Hormuz, where Kpler data show flows falling again, more than on US balances. WTI's storm premium is likely to be tested as Gulf of Mexico barrels return.

## 3. Key fundamental developments

**US weekly data (week to 9 Oct): a supply-side draw, not a demand signal.**
- **Crude stocks:** –8.6 mb{{F:crude_stocks_wchg}} to 415.6 mb{{F:crude_stocks}}, now 11.3 mb{{F:crude_stocks_vs_5yr}} (2.7%{{F:crude_stocks_vs_5yr_pct}}) below the 5-year average. That is a second straight draw, –11.7 mb{{F:crude_stocks_chg_2w}} over two weeks. Year-ago stocks were 423.8 mb{{S:wpsr}}.
- **Why it drew:**
  - Production fell to 13.68 mb/d{{F:production}} and imports to 6.05 mb/d{{F:imports}}. Both moves are consistent with the Isaias shut-ins of 1.28 mb/d{{S:ev_20261008_9}}.
  - Runs rose only 135 kb/d{{F:refinery_runs_wchg}}, to 16.6 mb/d{{F:refinery_runs}} (utilisation 93.5%{{F:utilisation}}).
  - Exports slipped 195 kb/d{{F:exports_wchg}}, which slightly reduced the draw.
  - Landfall was expected late 9 Oct or early 10 Oct, so storm effects, including any refinery disruption, may still show up next week.
- **Cushing:**
  - +1.86 mb{{F:cushing_stocks_wchg}} to 26.6 mb{{F:cushing_stocks}}; +5.1 mb{{F:cushing_stocks_chg_4w}} over four weeks.
  - Now 1.0 mb{{F:cushing_stocks_vs_5yr}} (3.9%{{F:cushing_stocks_vs_5yr_pct}}) above the 5-year average, having been below its norm a week ago.
  - The figure comes from a separate EIA API series, not the archived tables.
- **Products:**
  - Gasoline +3.5 mb{{F:gasoline_stocks_wchg}}, still 4.4%{{F:gasoline_stocks_vs_5yr_pct}} below average.
  - Distillate –2.5 mb{{F:distillate_stocks_wchg}} to 102.6 mb{{F:distillate_stocks}}. That is the fourth straight draw (4 weeks{{F:distillate_stocks_streak}}), leaving stocks 12.2%{{F:distillate_stocks_vs_5yr_pct}} below average.
  - Total commercial stocks fell 13.4 mb{{S:wpsr}}. The SPR was unchanged at 283.0 mb{{F:spr_stocks}}.
  - Product supplied rose 0.97 mb/d{{F:product_supplied_wchg}} to 21.36 mb/d{{F:product_supplied}}. This is one noisy week and could not be cross-checked against the archived tables.
- **Read:**
  - In our 2010–2024 study, the **level** of Cushing stocks is the strongest inventory link to WTI M1–M2: about $0.029/bbl{{S:analysis}} more backwardation per mb below normal. Weekly changes explain little.
  - With Cushing now above normal and still building, the Nov–Dec rebound is better read as a storm and front-month effect than a durable tightening. That is an inference, not a tested result.
  - Likewise, the study links a wide Brent–WTI to tight Cushing plus high exports. Cushing is no longer tight.

**Global supply and geopolitics: Brent's support has shifted to Hormuz.**
- **Kpler:**
  - Hormuz exports averaged 8.5 mb/d{{S:ev_20261014_12}} in the week to 12 Oct, nearly 40%{{S:ev_20261014_12}} below pre-war.
  - About a dozen{{S:ev_20261008_10}} tanker attacks hit between 28 Sep and 2 Oct.
  - A container ship sank on 14 Oct, the war's third{{S:ev_20261014_12}} commercial sinking (IMO).
- **IEA (Oct OMR):**
  - Gulf crude exports were 13.9 mb/d{{S:op_15}} in September, over 75%{{S:op_15}} of pre-war.
  - The two measures are not comparable, and the October reversal rests mainly on Kpler, so treat it as single-sourced.
  - EIA's STEO assumes Middle East shut-ins of 4.5 mb/d{{S:op_9}} in Q4.
- **Diplomacy:** On 9 Oct the President cited productive talks with Iran and pledged no attack before the 3 Nov midterms. Brent fell ~$1.2{{S:ev_20261009_11}} intraday but still settled +$0.44{{S:ev_20261009_11}}.
- **Global balance:**
  - IEA observed global stocks fell only 10 mb{{S:op_16}} in September. Behind that, on-land stocks fell 113 mb{{S:op_16}} (lowest since at least 2016), offset by oil on water +103 mb{{S:op_16}}.
  - IEA sees 2026 demand falling 2.6 mb/d{{S:op_14}}. OPEC still sees growth of 0.2 mb/d{{S:op_12}}.
- **China:**
  - September crude imports were 9.03 mb/d{{S:cn_6}} (–21.5%{{S:cn_6}} y/y).
  - Onshore draws were a record 57 mb / 47.5 mb{{S:cn_7}} (Vortexa / Kpler); these are unofficial estimates.
  - Product exports resumed on 9 Oct, but the October quota of ~3.7 Mt{{S:cn_8}} is below September's ~4.36 Mt{{S:cn_8}}.

**Products.**
- The crack collapse combines three things:
  - China's return to export markets;
  - the front-loaded G7 diesel release, up to 100 mb{{S:ev_20261002_5}} of crude and diesel;
  - the gasoline build.
- Part of it is mechanical: the WTI Nov leg rose $6.48{{F:wti_m1_wchg}}.
- Global runs are at a seasonal low of 81.3 mb/d{{S:op_17}} in October, which damps crude demand.

## 4. Trading implications

**Bullish**
- US crude is now below its 5-year average after a draw 8.6 mb{{F:crude_stocks_wchg}} larger than the headline expectation.
- Distillate is 12.2%{{F:distillate_stocks_vs_5yr_pct}} below average after four straight draws.
- Hormuz flows are falling and ships are sinking.
- Physical Brent is still $20.49{{F:brent_spot_premium}} over futures, and global on-land stocks are very low.

**Bearish**
- The storm draw should reverse as Gulf of Mexico output and imports recover.
- Cushing is above normal and has built for 4 weeks{{F:cushing_stocks_streak}}.
- Cracks fell sharply, squeezing refinery margins.
- Policy supply is coming: a 40 mb{{S:ev_20260929_2}} SPR exchange for Nov–Dec, the G7 release, and Chinese product exports.
- Demand estimates keep falling (IEA –2.6 mb/d{{S:op_14}}).

**Positioning view** (analytical, not a recommendation):
- **WTI structure:** The Nov–Dec rebound to $1.47{{F:wti_m1m2}} runs against Cushing's level. We see it as vulnerable once storm barrels return and Nov rolls off.
- **Brent–WTI ($9.42{{F:brent_wti_dec}}):** The storm-driven WTI premium could unwind and widen the spread again. Exports near 4.6 mb/d{{F:exports_avg_2026_10}} are likely to keep limiting how far it can widen. Hormuz is the main upside driver for Brent's leg.
- **Cracks:** Pressure has turned bearish, but distillate stocks are too low for a sustained collapse. Watch whether Chinese volumes come in below quota.
- **Flat price:** Brent is now driven by geopolitics, not US data. The Dec–Jan softening and the narrower physical premium argue against chasing the WTI rally (vol 36.6%{{F:wti_vol_20d}}).
