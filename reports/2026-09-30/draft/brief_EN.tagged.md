Even so, forward structure is softening in both benchmarks, most sharply in WTI as Cushing rebuilds, and US policy risk (a possible diesel export ban and a 40 mb{{S:ev_20260929_6}} SPR exchange) weighs on WTI more than on Brent.**

## 3. Key fundamental developments

- **The headline crude build is driven by refinery runs.** Commercial crude rose 0.9 mb{{F:crude_stocks_wchg}} to 427.3 mb{{F:crude_stocks}}, a second consecutive build (2{{F:crude_stocks_streak}} weeks; +3.9 mb{{F:crude_stocks_chg_2w}} over two weeks). Stocks stand 7.5 mb{{F:crude_stocks_vs_5yr}} (1.8%{{F:crude_stocks_vs_5yr_pct}}) above the 5-year average. Trade flows pointed the other way: imports fell 179 kb/d{{F:imports_wchg}} to 5.7 mb/d{{F:imports}} and exports rose 289 kb/d{{F:exports_wchg}} to 3.57 mb/d{{F:exports}}, and both changes would normally draw stocks. The build therefore traces to runs, which fell to 16.26 mb/d{{F:refinery_runs}} at 92.5%{{F:utilisation}} utilisation (−1.5 pp{{F:utilisation_wchg}}), against a 4-week average of 95.3%{{F:utilisation_4wk}}. The EIA gives no cause. Autumn maintenance is the usual explanation, but none of our sources confirms it. The SPR fell 0.8 mb{{F:spr_stocks_wchg}}.
- **Cushing is rebuilding, and the WTI spread is following.** Cushing stocks rose 0.55 mb{{F:cushing_stocks_wchg}} to 24.3 mb{{F:cushing_stocks}}, up 2.8 mb{{F:cushing_stocks_chg_2w}} over two weeks. They remain 1.4 mb{{F:cushing_stocks_vs_5yr}} (5.6%{{F:cushing_stocks_vs_5yr_pct}}) below the 5-year average. Our 2010–2024 study links WTI M1–M2 mainly to stock *levels* versus normal: seasonal deviations explain 36%{{S:analysis}} of spread variation, while weekly changes show R² 0.03{{S:analysis}}. Below-normal Cushing therefore still fits backwardation, but the slide in Nov–Dec coinciding with the rebuild is consistent with that relationship. These are associations, not causal estimates. The study's sensitivity (about $0.029/bbl{{S:analysis}} per mb below normal) explains only a small part of today's backwardation, which is mostly a global story.
- **Products are tight and the cracks show it.** Distillates fell 2.3 mb{{F:distillate_stocks_wchg}} to 105.2 mb{{F:distillate_stocks}}, 16.7 mb{{F:distillate_stocks_vs_5yr}} below the 5-year average. Gasoline fell 1.7 mb{{F:gasoline_stocks_wchg}} and sits 6.8%{{F:gasoline_stocks_vs_5yr_pct}} below normal. Total commercial petroleum stocks fell 7.0 mb{{S:wpsr}}. Demand looks firm:
  - Product supplied rose to 21.5 mb/d{{F:product_supplied}}.
  - Four-week distillate demand is up 5.2%{{S:wpsr}} y/y and jet up 6.5%{{S:wpsr}}.
  - Lower runs cut distillate output to 5.0 mb/d{{S:wpsr}}.

  The ULSD crack gained $4.03{{F:ulsd_crack_wchg}} even though diesel-ban headlines are bearish for US diesel in principle. We cannot separate whether the market is discounting a ban or simply pricing scarce stocks.
- **US policy risk is concentrated on WTI.**
  - *Diesel export ban.* President Trump backed keeping diesel at home (22 Sep). Politico reported a 90-day export-ban plan, which the White House denied as a flat ban; Oct ULSD fell about 4%{{S:ev_20260923_2}} that day. On 28 Sep, Trump was reported to be considering a ban 'very seriously'. Reporting is fragmented and nothing has been decided. A ban would likely cut Gulf Coast runs, and with them US crude demand.
  - *SPR exchange.* The DOE is soliciting a 40 mb{{S:ev_20260929_6}} SPR exchange of sour crude from Bryan Mound and Big Hill for Nov–Dec delivery. Uptake is uncertain: only about 0.5 mb{{S:ev_20260929_6}} of the June offer was taken.
- **Geopolitics gave two-way risk.** Houthi strikes (intercepted) and reports of US–Iran talks coincided with Brent Nov falling 2.1%{{S:ev_20260924_3}} to $104.30{{S:ev_20260924_3}} on 25 Sep. The US then rejected Iran's proposal to reopen Hormuz within 7 days, and Brent Nov rose about 3%{{S:ev_20260926_4}} in Asian trading on 28 Sep. Kpler puts Middle East exports at 12.8 mb/d{{S:ev_20260928_5}} in Sep, versus about 17 mb/d{{S:ev_20260928_5}} pre-war; this is a single source.
- **Global backdrop.**
  - The IEA reported global observed stocks fell 95 mb{{S:op_6}} in August, with 507 mb{{S:op_6}} drawn from Feb to Aug.
  - Demand forecasts diverge sharply: the IEA projects 2026 demand at −2.5 mb/d{{S:op_5}}, while OPEC projects +0.38 mb/d{{S:op_0}}.
  - China's August crude imports were 8.93 mb/d{{S:cn_0}}, against runs of 13.91 mb/d{{S:cn_1}}, implying a stock draw of 0.64 mb/d{{S:cn_2}}.
  - China's NBS manufacturing PMI was 50.1{{S:cn_4}}.

## 4. Trading implications

**Bullish**
- Prompt physical scarcity: the Nov–Dec expiry spread was $5.50{{F:brent_expiring_vs_m1}} and the Brent spot assessment stood $11.37{{F:brent_spot_premium}} over futures.
- The diplomatic path on Hormuz has been pushed back, and Middle East exports remain well below pre-war levels.
- US distillate stocks are 13.7%{{F:distillate_stocks_vs_5yr_pct}} below normal while demand is firm, and the IEA shows large global draws.
- China's stock draws may eventually require restocking imports; this is speculative.

**Bearish**
- WTI curve structure is unwinding as Cushing rebuilds, and WTI Dec/Dec fell $3.05{{F:wti_zz_wchg}}.
- A diesel export ban and the SPR exchange are both direct risks to US crude demand and Gulf Coast sour balances.
- Middle East flows are recovering, and Brent Dec/Dec eased $1.95{{F:brent_zz_wchg}}.
- The IEA's demand contraction points to demand destruction.

**Positioning view (analytical, not a recommendation):** The market is pricing a two-tier picture. The physical prompt is extremely tight, while deferred spreads are deflating. WTI looks more exposed than Brent because three US-specific risks are stacking up: Cushing builds, the SPR exchange, and the diesel-ban threat. Our study associates a wide Brent–WTI spread with tight Cushing and high exports, but this widening instead came with Cushing building and exports below the April–May averages of 5.15–5.23 mb/d{{F:exports_avg_2026_04}}{{F:exports_avg_2026_05}}. That makes it look like WTI-specific weakness, which a policy reversal could unwind quickly. With 20-day volatility at 44.8%{{F:wti_vol_20d}}, headline risk on Hormuz and the diesel ban dominates single-week inventory signals.
